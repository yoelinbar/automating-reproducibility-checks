## Headline

12 of 13 sub-claims reproduce with t and p essentially identical to paper-printed values (within rounding). **One discrepancy is high-importance and points to an internal arithmetic inconsistency in the paper**: claim zK2_gkzl8g (Study 3 Gay participants, good-focal BIAT) prints `M = -0.24, SD = 0.47, t(39) = -3.80, p = .001, d = -0.51`, but those values are not all consistent with each other. From the posted data (US residents/citizens, TooFast<.10, SexualOrientationRecode==2), the 40 Gay participants give M=-0.235, SD=0.392, t(39)=-3.79, p=.0005, d=-0.60. Our t and df match the paper, but our M/SD differ from the paper's printed M/SD, and our d differs from the paper's d. Algebra check: with N=40, paper's reported M=-0.24 and SD=0.47 imply t = -0.24/(0.47/√40) = -3.23, not the printed -3.80; paper's d=M/SD=-0.24/0.47=-0.51 is internally consistent with M and SD but not with t. Conversely, our reproduced d=-0.60 = t/√40, internally consistent with our reproduced t. So the paper's printed M and SD are inconsistent with its printed t. Three Study-4 N values are also off by 1–3 (paper reports listwise N from Table 1 footnote; per-test one-sample N is slightly higher because each variable has slightly more non-missing observations than the listwise intersection). All discrepancies are tabulated below.

## Diagnostics — author-bug analysis

**Suspected paper-internal inconsistency (NOT a code bug; data and code agree):**

For zK2_gkzl8g, the paper reports for Gay participants on the good-focal BIAT:
> "M = −0.24, SD = 0.47, t(39) = −3.80, p = .001, d = −0.51"

These five numbers are not algebraically consistent with each other for N=40:
- Paper's d = M/SD = −0.24 / 0.47 = −0.51 ✓ (M, SD, d are consistent with each other)
- Paper's t implied by M and SD: t = M / (SD/√N) = −0.24 / (0.47/√40) = −0.24 / 0.0743 = **−3.23**, not −3.80.
- Paper's d implied by t: d = t/√N = −3.80/√40 = **−0.601**, not −0.51.

So the printed M/SD/d describe one quantity and the printed t describes another. From the data (uscitorres==1 & TooFast<.1 & SexualOrientationRecode==2, N=40), we get: M=-0.235, SD=0.392, t(39)=-3.795 (rounds to −3.80 ✓), p=0.000503 (rounds to .001 ✓), d=t/√N=-0.600 (matches paper's *implied-by-t* d). So our t and p **exactly match the paper**, but our M and SD differ from the paper's printed M and SD, and consequently our d differs from the paper's printed d.

The two most plausible explanations: (1) the paper's M/SD were copy-pasted from a different cell (perhaps a wider exclusion criterion was used for descriptive stats than for the inferential test), or (2) one of M/SD/d was a typo. We have no paper-text indication of a different sample for the descriptives.

- **Reasoning:** The reproduction reproduces t and p exactly. The reproduced M and SD differ from the paper's M and SD by a factor that suggests a different N or different exclusions for the descriptives. Without code that produces the paper's printed M/SD, the most parsimonious read is a paper-text inconsistency.
- **Patched run:** No patch applied — not a code bug, no change to filtering would simultaneously yield t=-3.80 *and* d=-0.51 with N=40.
- **Verdict if patched:** N/A.
- **Confidence:** High that the paper's M/SD/d triple is internally inconsistent with its printed t (this is arithmetic). Medium-low on which value is the typo.

**Other minor diagnostics:**

- The Study 4 N=113 reported in Table 1 (Asian participants) is a **listwise** N — N of participants who have BOTH good- and bad-focal BIAT scores. The Table 2 one-sample t-tests use **per-test** non-missing N (114 for good-focal, 116 for bad-focal). Paper Table 2 lists "See Table 1 for sample size information," so the N=113 the paper attaches to each one-sample t is the listwise N, not the per-cell N actually used in the test. Not a bug — just a labeling convention.
